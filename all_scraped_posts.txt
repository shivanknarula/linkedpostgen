Humanoid robots need to move through human environments, but whole body locomotion over obstacles remains challenging. The robot must coordinate its legs, arms, torso and contacts while reacting to terrain in real time. In this work, we train a whole body humanoid locomotion controller by combining an online motion generation and motion tracking for traversing diverse types of terrains. Given the robot state, terrain height scan and target direction, the diffusion based motion generator predicts a short horizon to rein aware reference motion. The royal tracker then converts this reference into stable joint actions for the real robot. We first build a whole body motion data set by retargeting human motion data to the robot. Using this offline data set we retrain a motion tracker and a motion generator. However, directly combining the pre trained generator and tracker can still be brittle due to imperfect generated references and limited terrain diversity during pre training. We therefore fine tune the tracker with the frozen generator using RL on more diverse terrains and direction commands enabling. Table real time execution with generated references. After fine tuning, the system can robustly handle terrain geometry changes using online generated trajectories. The full system is deployed onboard a unitary G1 humanoid using Lidar based elevation mapping and real time onboard inference. On hardware, the robot successfully climbs onto high boxes and jumps down from different directions and configurations. It also vaults over hurdles and traverses stairs, demonstrating the ability to switch between different locomotion skills. Our system also shows local navigation capability. When the robot is not well positioned to traverse an obstacle directly, it adapts online and bypasses it while still moving toward the target. Finally, the robot completes mixed terrain sequences that combine multiple obstacle types within a single run. Together, these results show that our framework can generate and execute adaptive whole body motions for diverse real-world terrain traversal tasks.

Successfully Completed a 5-Day ROS 2 Workshop!
I recently had the opportunity to participate in an 5-day ROS 2 (Robot Operating System) workshop
During the workshop, I explored:
🔹 ROS 2 fundamentals and architecture
🔹 Linux, ROS 2 workspace and package creation
🔹 Publisher, Subscriber, Services and Actions
🔹 Robot simulation using Turtlesim, Gazebo and RViz2
🔹 Robot design concepts using Fusion 360 and URDF
🔹 SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) and Nav2
🔹 LiDAR integration and mapping
🔹 ROS 2 communication with microcontrollers
🔹 Introduction to Computer Vision with OpenCV and ROS 2
One of the most exciting parts was visualizing robots in Gazebo, creating maps using LiDAR, and understanding how autonomous navigation works in real-world robotics applications.
As someone who was experiencing ROS 2 for the first time, this workshop genuinely sparked my interest in robotics and autonomous systems. The hands-on sessions, practical demonstrations, and interactive discussions made the learning experience both engaging and enjoyable.
A big thank you to Aryan Jagushte sir and the Robotry team for an insightful learning experience. Special thanks to our HOD, Chandan M N Sir, for his constant support and encouragement, and to @ARSA for organizing this wonderful workshop and making it a great learning opportunity for students.
Looking forward to learning more and building exciting projects in Robotics, ROS 2, Computer Vision, and Autonomous Systems.
