1d
Excited to share a project that pushed us outside our comfort zone during the Intrinsic AI for Industry Challenge (AIC). 🤖
The challenge: train a robot to autonomously insert cable connectors into target ports — a manipulation problem requiring sub-millimeter precision, contact-aware behavior, and generalization across randomized environments.
Over the past few weeks, I worked alongside Narendar Sriram, Sai Shruthi Balaji, Sharon Richu S. and Varun Gampa to build an end-to-end imitation learning pipeline for precision robotic manipulation: inserting SFP and SC plugs into randomized ports on a task board.
We didn’t qualify for the next phase — but that isn’t the takeaway.
Coming from robotics, controls, and AI backgrounds, this was an opportunity to apply those fundamentals to learning-based manipulation and understand firsthand where robot learning succeeds — and where it breaks.
What we built:
🔧 Custom LeRobot integration
Integrated ROS 2 control, multi-camera observations, and a 26D robot state directly into the training and data collection pipeline.
📡 Automated demonstration collection
Built a passive observer pipeline that recorded ground-truth CheatCode actions as demonstrations, enabling consistent, low-noise supervision without manual teleoperation.
📐 SE(3) relative action representation
Encoded actions as relative pose deltas instead of absolute targets, improving robustness to randomized board positions and reducing dependence on workspace coordinates.
🎬 Scalable dataset infrastructure
Built an automated collection and curation pipeline covering hundreds of demonstrations and ~363K frames, with tooling to inspect trajectories and surgically remove failed episodes at the parquet + video level without re-recording datasets.
🧠 ACT policy training
Trained Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) using multi-view observations and robot state for sequential manipulation.
Three lessons that stood out:
1. Simulation fidelity matters.
Physics quality and roll-out throughput directly affect learning outcomes.
2. Clean data beats more data.
Consistent demonstrations mattered more than collecting additional episodes.
3. Diversity beats repetition.
Interleaving environments improved generalization far more than collecting many samples per configuration.
This challenge sharpened our understanding of the full robot learning stack — data, representation, training, and deployment.
Huge thanks to the Intrinsic team for creating a genuinely challenging and well-designed competition.
